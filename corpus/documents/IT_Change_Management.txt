# Change Management Policy

## 1. Purpose

This policy controls how changes reach production so that reliability is not traded for speed. It applies to application deploys, infrastructure changes, database migrations, configuration changes and third-party integrations. Documentation-only changes and changes made entirely inside a feature-flagged path that is off in production are exempt.

## 2. Change types

1. **Standard** — pre-approved, low-risk changes with a documented runbook, such as a routine dependency patch or a dashboard update.
2. **Normal** — any change that is not standard or emergency; requires review and a change record.
3. **Emergency** — a change needed to resolve an active Sev-1 or Sev-2. Emergency changes may proceed with verbal approval from the incident commander and are recorded retrospectively within one business day.

## 3. Normal change process

1. Open a change record describing the change, the systems affected, the rollback plan and the monitoring plan.
2. Obtain code review from at least one engineer who is not the author.
3. Obtain approval from the system owner. Changes touching payment flows also require Finance sign-off.
4. Submit to the Change Advisory Board (CAB) at least 5 business days before the planned deployment window.
5. Deploy in the approved window and watch the dashboards for 30 minutes.
6. Close the change record with the observed outcome.

The CAB meets every Tuesday at 15:00 IST. Changes submitted after the Monday 12:00 cut-off are considered the following week. A change rejected by the CAB may be re-submitted once with the requested amendments.

## 4. Deployment windows

Production deploys are permitted Monday to Thursday, 10:00 to 17:00 IST. Friday deploys require the engineering director's approval. No production changes are made during the end-of-quarter freeze, from the 25th of the last month of each quarter until the second working day of the next quarter, except for emergency changes and security patches.

## 5. Rollback

Every normal change must have a tested rollback plan. Database migrations must be backward compatible for at least one release so the application can roll back without restoring data. A change without a rollback plan is rejected by the CAB.

## 6. Post-deployment

1. Monitor error rates, latency and saturation for at least 30 minutes.
2. If the change is implicated in an incident, roll back first and investigate afterwards.
3. Changes that cause an incident require a postmortem under IT_Incident_Response_Runbook.

## 7. Exceptions

An exception to the window or the CAB requirement needs the engineering director's written approval and an expiry date. Exceptions are reviewed monthly by Reliability Engineering.
