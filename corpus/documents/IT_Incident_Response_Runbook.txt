# Incident Response Runbook

## 1. Severity definitions

1. **Sev-1** — customer-facing outage or data loss affecting more than 20% of customers, or any confirmed security breach.
2. **Sev-2** — degraded service affecting a material subset of customers, or an internal system blocking a revenue process.
3. **Sev-3** — limited impact with a workaround.
4. **Sev-4** — cosmetic or single-user issue.

Severity is declared by the on-call engineer and may be raised by the incident commander at any time. Downgrading a Sev-1 requires the incident commander and the engineering director.

## 2. Response windows

| Step | Sev-1 | Sev-2 |
|---|---|---|
| Acknowledge page | 15 minutes | 1 hour |
| Open incident channel | 15 minutes | 1 hour |
| Status page update | 30 minutes | 2 hours |
| Customer communication | 1 hour | 4 hours |

Missing an acknowledgement window is tracked as a reliability metric, not a disciplinary matter, unless it is repeated.

## 3. Roles

The incident commander owns the incident and does not debug. The communications lead writes status updates. The subject-matter engineers investigate. The scribe keeps the timeline. For a Sev-1, the incident commander is the on-call engineering manager; for a Sev-2 it may be the on-call engineer.

## 4. Process

1. Acknowledge the page and declare severity in the incident channel.
2. Open the bridge. For a Sev-1, the bridge must open within 15 minutes of declaration; see Operations_Sev_Incident_Escalation for the current runbook version.
3. Assign the four roles.
4. Mitigate first, diagnose second. Roll back if the change is the suspected cause.
5. Update the status page at the intervals in Section 2.
6. Resolve only when the customer impact has ended, not when the root cause is understood.

## 5. Communication

Internal updates go in the incident channel every 30 minutes for a Sev-1 and every 2 hours for a Sev-2. Customer updates are written by the communications lead and approved by the incident commander. Never speculate about root cause in a customer update.

## 6. Postmortem

1. A postmortem is required for every Sev-1 and Sev-2.
2. It is due within 5 business days of resolution.
3. It is blameless: it names systems and decisions, not individuals.
4. It contains a timeline, contributing factors, what went well, and action items with owners and dates.
5. Action items are tracked in the reliability backlog and reviewed monthly.
