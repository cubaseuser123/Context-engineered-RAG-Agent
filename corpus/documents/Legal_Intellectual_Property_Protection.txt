# Intellectual Property Protection

## 1. Ownership

Work created by an employee in the course of employment belongs to Meridian. Employment contracts assign all inventions, designs, code and content to the company. Contractors must sign an assignment before starting work; work from a contractor without an assignment must not be merged into a product.

## 2. Invention disclosure

1. Employees must disclose an invention to Legal within 30 days of conceiving it, before any public disclosure.
2. The disclosure describes the problem, the solution, prior art known to the inventor and any planned publication.
3. Legal decides within 45 days whether to file, hold or release the invention.
4. Public disclosure before filing can destroy patentability, so conference talks and blog posts about unreleased work require Legal review.

## 3. Open source

1. Using open source requires a licence review for anything outside the approved list.
2. Copyleft licences (GPL, AGPL) require Legal approval before use in a distributed product.
3. Every dependency addition is recorded in the software bill of materials.
4. Employees must not copy code from the internet into the product without a licence record.

## 4. Trademarks and brand

The Meridian name and logo are registered trademarks. Use follows the brand guidelines; modified logos and new product names require Legal clearance. Domain names are registered centrally, not by individual teams.

## 5. Confidential information

Trade secrets must be protected under IT_Data_Classification_Standard. NDAs are required before sharing technical details with a prospective partner. The NDA template is pre-approved; changes need Legal review.

## 6. Third-party IP

Using a third party's content, images or data requires a licence or an exception. Screenshots of competitor products in marketing require Legal review. Customer logos require written consent.
